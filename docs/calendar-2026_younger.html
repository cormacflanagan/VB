How to read it, and what it is not

  • This is a field, not a year group. The cohort is everyone graduating in 2026 or later, because that is who an 18U draw admits. 30 of the top 60 graduate in 2026 itself; the rest are younger girls playing up, and they are the reason the field is harder than a single class would suggest.
  • Turnout counts athletes, not teams. A number is how many of that tier entered that bracket; a girl who played both the 18U and the 17U is counted in both rows, which is the point of splitting them.
  • Brackets are folded onto the age they are actually played at. Organisers label one field a dozen ways — "Girls 18U", "U18 Girls", "Girls 18:U (Grad Year 2026-2027)" and "Class of '26 & Younger" are the same competition. An explicit age wins; failing that the youngest graduating year admitted sets the ceiling.
  • Doubles only. Club, 3v3 and 5v5 entries are excluded throughout: a placing in those says little about an individual.
  • A blank in the last column means "not posted", not "not happening". Only 11 of the 232 events this field entered have a new edition listed yet. Matching is by name after stripping years and ordinals, so a renamed event misses too.
  • The forward schedule is as complete as Volleyball Life is. It is rebuilt by walking tournament ids rather than read from a listing feed, so an event appears the day its organiser creates it there — and an event run off Volleyball Life does not appear at all. FIVB and international dates are out of scope.
  • The training series are not tournaments. They are invitational residential camps with no draw and no result, so Volleyball Life has no record of them; dates and rosters come from USA Volleyball. Their turnout columns count selections, not entries, and are not comparable with a tournament's.
  • Locations come from the tournament record and are blank where the organiser left them unset, which is common for one-day local events.
