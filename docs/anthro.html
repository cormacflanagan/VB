The whole field

Height against weight for every woman who has played Olympic beach volleyball and has both numbers published. The shaded column is the 172–178 cm band — Haisley's height, give or take an inch.

Senior Olympics, 180 women Youth Olympics (ages 16–18), 50 women Haisley, 5′9″ and 146 lb

Weight tracks height closely in this population — the senior fit is 0.68 kg per centimetre with a correlation of 0.73, so a player one inch taller is expected about 3.8 lb heavier. Read straight off that line, the expected weight at 5′9″ is 145 lb. Haisley is at 146.

What the chart also shows is how far below the middle of the field her height sits. The senior women average 179 cm (5′10″) and run up to 196 cm — Bibiana Candelas of Mexico. Every point to the right of her band is a player she would have to hit over.

Inside the band

The 60 senior women and 21 Youth Olympians who stand 172–178 cm, by weight. Bars are share of their own group, because the two samples are different sizes; the bar under each axis is the middle half, with the median marked.

Bins are 2 kg wide. Every athlete is counted once, at the weight published for her.

Among senior Olympians her height the median is 148 lb (67 kg) and the middle half runs 141 to 154 lb. Haisley at 146 lb sits at the 47th percentile — the centre of the distribution, not the edge of it. On BMI the same thing holds: her 21.6 against a band median of 21.7 and a middle half of 20.5 to 22.5.

Against the age-matched group the reading changes. Youth Olympic women her height — 16 to 18 years old, the same age as she is — have a median of 141 lb (64 kg), and she is at the 71st percentile of them. She is already carrying a senior athlete's weight at a junior athlete's age.

The heaviest senior Olympian in the band weighed 77 kg (170 lb). A year of gain at half a pound a week reaches 172 lb, which is past her — there is no woman in the Olympic record at this height carrying that weight. Three months gets to roughly the top of the middle half; six months is past it.

This is a distribution, not a target. Elite adults are where these players ended up after years of adult training, not a weight a 16-year-old should be steered toward. What the chart supports is a narrow claim: the gap that a weight-gain phase exists to close is closed. It says nothing about how to taper, and nothing about body composition — a scale cannot separate muscle from the rest, and that distinction is the whole question. That belongs with a sports dietitian who works with adolescent athletes.

How this was built, and what it cannot tell you

  • Source. Olympedia, scraped edition by edition: the beach volleyball tournament at every Summer Games from 1996 to 2024 and at the Youth Olympic Games of 2014 and 2018, then each competitor's biography page. That is 714 athletes in all, 351 of them women, of whom 230 have both a height and a weight on file.
  • The measurements are self-reported and undated. They come from the entry form an athlete's federation filed for a given Games and are not revised afterwards, so a player who competed in four Olympics carries one number for all four. Expect them to be flattering and a little stale. They are consistent enough for a distribution and too soft for any single comparison.
  • Missing data is not random. 121 women in the record have no measurements published, weighted toward smaller federations and the earlier Games. Nothing here corrects for that.
  • Olympians are the extreme tail. This is the top of the professional game, not the population Haisley competes against now or will at college. It answers "what does this body type look like at the very top", not "what should a junior weigh".
  • The Youth Olympic sample is small. 21 women in the band. It is the only age-matched Olympic-grade comparison available, and it should be read as an indication rather than a measurement.
  • Points are jittered. Heights and weights are published as whole numbers, so many athletes share a coordinate. Each point is nudged by up to 0.3 units, derived from the athlete's own id so it never moves between runs, purely so overlapping players stay countable. Every number quoted in the text uses the unjittered values.
